## 📈 Markets & Economics

**Inflation Is Heating Up — TIPS Funds in Focus**
The Daily Upside flags rising inflation and highlights TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) funds. This isn't noise — it's a regime signal. **The macro picture:** We're in a world where fiscal deficits are structural (not cyclical), deglobalization is inflationary, and energy volatility from the Iran situation adds supply-side pressure. The Fed is boxed in: cutting rates into inflation risks a dollar crisis, but holding rates tightens financial conditions. TIPS are a hedge, not a conviction — they protect purchasing power but don't generate real returns if inflation moderates.

Watch companies in power management, cooling, and electrical infrastructure — they're the picks-and-shovels play of the next AI bottleneck.

## 📚 Macroeconomics: How the World Economy Really Works

**Why This Specific Aspect Matters:**
Today we're tackling **global capital flows** — the invisible rivers of money that move between countries every second. This matters right now because the US dollar's strength, rising tariffs, and shifting trade alliances are reshaping where money goes and who benefits. Understanding this helps you see why your investments in tech stocks or real estate react to events in Tokyo or Frankfurt, not just Wall Street.

**The Core Concept (5-Minute Explainer):**
Imagine the world economy as a giant bathtub. Water (money) naturally flows from where it's abundant to where it's scarce — chasing higher returns. When US interest rates rise, money flows INTO the US (dollar strengthens). When rates drop, money flows OUT (dollar weakens). 

**Concrete example:** In 2022-2023, the Fed raised rates aggressively. Money flooded into US bonds, the dollar surged, and emerging markets suffered — their currencies weakened, imports got expensive, and foreign investors pulled capital out. This is why your international ETFs underperformed US stocks during that period.

The key insight: **capital flows create winners and losers before trade balances even matter.**

**The Counterintuitive Truth:**
Most people think trade deficits are "bad" and surpluses are "good." Reality? The US runs persistent trade deficits BECAUSE the world wants to invest here — our capital account surplus offsets the trade deficit. China's trade surplus means they're exporting goods but often reinvesting profits back into US assets. The deficit is a feature, not a bug, of being the world's reserve currency.

**3 Resources (Progressively Deep):**
1. 📖 Entry Point: *"The Dollar Trap" by Eswar Prasad** — Accessible, explains why the dollar stays dominant despite deficits
2. 🎯 Deep Dive: *"International Economics: Theory and Policy" by Krugman & Obstfeld** — The standard textbook, chapters on capital mobility
3. 🔬 Expert Level: *BIS (Bank for International Settlements) Quarterly Review** — Raw data on actual capital flows, free online
