## 🤖 AI & Tech Developments

**1. Railway Raises $100M Series B to Challenge AWS**
Railway, a San Francisco cloud platform, hit 2M developers with zero marketing spend and just closed $100M in Series B funding. The pitch: AI-native cloud infrastructure purpose-built for deploying AI workloads, not retrofitted from legacy compute. **Why it matters:** The cloud wars are entering a new phase. AWS, Azure, and GCP were built for web apps; Railway is betting that AI-native infrastructure — where GPU provisioning, model serving, and agent orchestration are first-class primitives — is a generational wedge. Watch for IPO timing in 18-24 months. If Railway captures even 5% of AI workloads migrating off hyperscalers, this is a multi-billion dollar outcome. **Investor angle:** No public ticker yet, but this validates the "picks and shovels" thesis for AI infrastructure. Companies like CoreWeave (CRWV) and GPU cloud providers benefit from the same tailwind.

**2. Goose Emerges as Free Alternative to Claude Code ($200/mo)**
Goose, an open-source AI coding agent, replicates much of Claude Code's terminal-based autonomous coding capability at zero cost. **Why it matters:** This is the open-source disruption pattern playing out in real time — exactly what happened to proprietary databases (PostgreSQL vs. Oracle) and IDEs (VS Code eating JetBrains' lunch). Anthropic's moat on Claude Code is narrowing. For investors, this pressures Anthropic's pricing power and reinforces that the value in AI is shifting *up the stack* — from models to applications and workflows. Companies building proprietary workflows and data moats (not just wrapping an API) will win.

**3. Salesforce Rebuilds Slackbot as Full AI Agent**
Salesforce transformed Slackbot from a notification tool into a full AI agent capable of enterprise search and task execution. **Why it matters:** This is Salesforce's answer to Microsoft's Copilot integration across Teams/Office. The workplace AI battleground is now the *messaging layer* — whoever owns the chat interface owns the workflow. Salesforce stock has been under pressure; this is a credible counterpunch, but execution risk is high. Watch Q3 earnings for any signal that this drives seat expansion or reduced churn.

**4. Listen Labs Raises $69M for AI Customer Interviews**
Listen Labs raised $69M after a viral billboard hiring stunt. The product: AI-driven customer interview automation at scale. **Why it matters:** This is a microcosm of the AI labor arbitrage theme. If AI can replace $150K/year UX researchers with $50K/year AI-augmented workflows, the TAM is enormous. The $69M raise at this stage signals VC conviction that "AI-native market research" is a category being born. Watch for public-market analogues in the customer insight space.

## 🌍 Geopolitics & Supply Chains

**Iran: The Standoff That Won't Resolve**
Trump called off a planned Tuesday attack, Pakistan is mediating, but Reuters reports "no deal, no exit." This is the most dangerous kind of geopolitical situation — an open-ended standoff with no off-ramp. **Investment implications:**

- **Energy:** Long oil, long energy equities. Every day of uncertainty adds $2-5/bbl of risk premium. ConocoPhillips (COP) and Canadian Natural Resources (CNQ) are the cleanest dividend-paying exposures.
- **Defense:** Lockheed Martin (LMT), Northrop Grumman (NOC), and Raytheon (RTX) benefit from any escalation. Even without strikes, the *threat* drives allied defense spending. NATO members are already accelerating procurement.
- **Shipping/Red Sea:** The Gulf's African aftershocks (proxy conflicts around the Red Sea and Horn of Africa) mean shipping disruption is not an "if" but a "when." Maersk and container shipping rates are the canary.

## 📚 Macroeconomics: How the World Economy Really Works

**Why This Specific Aspect Matters:**
Today we're tackling **how money actually moves through the global economy** — not the textbook circular flow diagram version, but the real plumbing. Understanding this is critical right now because the Federal Reserve's rate decisions, the strength of the dollar, and global capital flows directly impact your portfolio's performance. If you don't understand the mechanism, you're trading blind.

**The Core Concept (5-Minute Explainer):**
Forget "supply and demand" for a moment. Think of the global economy as a **plumbing system** — money flows like water through pipes (banks, markets, trade channels), and central banks control the pressure.

Here's the key: **most "money" isn't cash — it's credit.** When a bank makes a loan, it doesn't move existing money — it *creates* new money. This is how 90%+ of the money supply enters existence. When banks lend more, the economy expands. When they contract (like in 2008, or regional bank stress in 2023), money literally disappears.

**Concrete example:** When the Fed raised rates to 5.25-5.50% through 2022-2023, borrowing costs skyrocketed. Mortgage rates hit 7%+. That didn't just affect homebuyers — it tightened credit across the entire system. Businesses couldn't expand, hiring slowed, and the "money" in circulation shrank. That's why your tech stocks got hammered: less money flowing = less growth expectations.

**The Counterintuitive Truth:**
Everyone thinks inflation is "too much money chasing too few goods." That's partially true, but the real driver is **velocity** — how fast money changes hands. During COVID, the Fed printed trillions, but velocity *crashed* because people weren't spending. Inflation didn't spike immediately — it came later when velocity returned while supply chains were broken. The popular narrative got the timing wrong because they ignored velocity.

**3 Resources (Progressively Deep):**
1. 📖 **Entry Point:** *The Ascent of Money* by Niall Ferguson — A readable history of how money, credit, and banking evolved. Connects the dots from ancient coinage to modern derivatives.
2. 🎯 **Deep Dive:** *Where Does Money Come From?* by Josh Ryan-Collins et al. — Explains the actual mechanics of money creation by banks (not central banks). Eye-opening.
3. 🔬 **Expert Level:** *The Credit Theory of Money* by A. Mitchell Innes (1914 paper) — The original academic framework that modern MMT and post-Keynesian economics builds on. Dense but foundational.

**Today's Question to Sit With:**
If most money is created by private banks through lending, what happens to the economy when banks decide to stop lending — not because of regulation, but because of fear?

**Practical Connection:**
Watch the **TED Spread** (Treasury-Eurodollar spread) this week. It measures perceived credit risk in the banking system. When it widens, banks are nervous. When it tightens, confidence returns. It's a real-time pulse check on the plumbing. You can find it on FRED or Bloomberg. If it spikes, your portfolio positioning should shift toward quality and liquidity.
