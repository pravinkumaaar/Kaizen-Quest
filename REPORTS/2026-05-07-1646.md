## 🧠 FRAMEWORK OF THE DAY: KELLY CRITERION

I want to leave you with one framework to carry into every trading decision:

**Kelly Criterion tells you the OPTIMAL fraction of your bankroll to allocate:**

**f* = (bp - q) / b**

Where:
- b = odds received (reward/risk ratio)
- p = probability of winning
- q = probability of losing (1 - p)

**Example from today's NVDA trade:**
- Reward = 24%, Risk = 10% → b = 2.4
- Win probability = 70% → p = 0.70, q = 0.30
- f* = (2.4 × 0.70 - 0.30) / 2.4 = (1.68 - 0.30) / 2.4 = **0.575**

So the *mathematically optimal* allocation to NVDA is **57.5%** of your portfolio.

Does that mean you should put 57.5% into NVDA? **Absolutely not.** This is why practical position sizing uses **Half-KKelly** (28.75%) at maximum. Even that is aggressive. The lesson: Kelly tells you the CEILING, not the target. Your 13.5% in NVDA is actually well-calibrated.

**The real power of Kelly:** It tells you when to bet BIG (high win prob, good odds) and when to bet SMALL (low win prob, mediocre odds). PATH at 58% win probability with 2.4:1 odds gives f* = (2.4 × 0.58 - 0.42) / 2.4 = 0.405. Half-Kelly = 20%. But since this is a pre-earnings binary event, cap at 5% (options overlay). Discipline > formulas.

**The Core Concept (5-Minute Explainer):**

Longevity Escape Velocity (LEV) is the idea that if science can extend your life faster than you're aging, you effectively never die of old age. Not immortality — you can still get hit by a bus — but aging itself becomes a solved engineering problem.

Here's how to think about it. Right now, science gives you roughly 1 year of additional life expectancy for every 1 year you live. That's why, statistically, you creep toward mortality: each year you gain a little, but you're also losing a little.

Flip that ratio to 1.05: for every year you live, science gives you 1.05 extra years of healthy life. Now you're compounding. Not linearly — exponentially. After 10 years of living at 1.05x escape velocity, the math cascades. You don't just add 0.5 years — each year's gain stacks on previous gains, because the science keeps improving each year.

Aubrey de Grey, who coined the term, used a vivid analogy: imagine you're walking toward a cliff edge (death). Every year you take a step forward. Right now, medicine gives you half a step back. LEV means you get a full step back. You never reach the cliff. The cliff keeps moving away faster than you're walking toward it.

Think of it like compound interest: $10,000 at 7% doubles every 10 years. But compound *relative* to your own mortality? That's an entirely different curve.

Everyone assumes longevity = living to 120 looking 120. The reality is radically different.
