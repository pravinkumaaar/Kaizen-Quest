## 🤖 AI & Tech Developments

**1. Railway raises $100M to challenge AWS with AI-native cloud infrastructure**
Railway — a cloud platform that grew to 2M developers with zero marketing spend — closed a $100M Series B. The signal: AI-native infrastructure is the next layer of cloud competition. AWS, GCP, and Azure were built for general compute. Railway is purpose-built for AI workloads (GPU orchestration, model serving, agent deployment). For investors, this validates the thesis that the AI stack is creating entirely new infrastructure winners, not just extending old ones. Watch Railway's trajectory — if it captures even 5% of AI-native workloads, it becomes a meaningful platform.

**2. Goose offers Claude Code's capabilities for free**
Claude Code costs up to $200/month. Goose replicates similar AI coding-agent functionality at zero cost. This is the open-source disruption playbook applied to AI tooling. The implication: AI coding assistants are rapidly commoditizing. Anthropic's moat in developer tools is thinner than it appears. For investors, this pressures the "premium AI tooling" thesis — companies charging high prices for coding AI agents face a race to the bottom. Open-source alternatives will compress margins.

**3. Salesforce rebuilds Slackbot as a full AI agent**
Salesforce is transforming Slackbot from a notification tool into an enterprise AI agent. This is Salesforce's answer to Microsoft Copilot's deep Office integration. The battleground is now workplace AI — who owns the interface layer between workers and AI. Salesforce is betting Slack becomes that layer. Risk: Slack's enterprise adoption has plateaued, and Microsoft's distribution advantage is enormous.

**4. Listen Labs raises $69M for AI customer interviews**
Listen Labs uses AI to conduct and analyze customer interviews at scale. The $69M raise signals that "AI-native research" is a real category. For investors, this is a second-order AI play — not infrastructure, but application-layer disruption of market research, UX research, and customer intelligence.

## 📈 Markets & Economics

**The macro picture is dominated by one thing: the Iran conflict and its energy shock.**

- **Strait of Hormuz closure** is the single biggest market risk right now. UBS warns global oil stockpiles could hit record lows by end of May. Diesel costs are already straining US school budgets — that's a real-economy transmission mechanism, not just a headline.
- **Kevin Warsh is the new Fed Chair**, and he's walking into a nightmare: inflation spiking (energy-driven), Treasury yields surging, and an FOMC in no mood to cut. This is the exact opposite of the "rate cuts incoming" narrative that markets priced in earlier this year. Stagflation risk is real — rising prices with slowing growth.
- **Trump says the Iran war is "worth the economic pain"** and rural voters agree. This tells you the administration is not going to blink on energy prices. No strategic Petroleum Reserve releases, no diplomatic off-ramps that look like capitulation. Energy inflation is a feature, not a bug, of current policy.
- **Social Security Trustees' report is delayed**, and when it arrives, it may show insolvency sooner than expected. Combined with the DEI backlash threatening the Rooney Rule and state auto-IRAs crossing $3B in assets, the retirement savings landscape is shifting under people's feet.

**Investment implication:** Energy is the macro trade. Not just oil — diesel, refining margins, and energy infrastructure. The Fed is boxed in: can't cut into energy-driven inflation, can't hike into a war economy. Cash and short-duration bonds are the defensive play. Equities face a margin squeeze as input costs rise and demand softens.

**Iran is the center of gravity.**

- **Strait of Hormuz closure** threatens ~20% of global oil transit. Iran's stock market reopens Tuesday — expect massive volatility. The market has been closed during the worst of the conflict; pent-up selling pressure will be significant.

## 📚 Macroeconomics: How the World Economy Really Works

**Why This Specific Aspect Matters:**
Today we're tackling the single most misunderstood engine of the global economy: **how money actually moves — not through textbooks, but through trust, power, and human behavior. This matters right now because every investment decision you make (AI stocks, energy plays, bonds) depends on understanding that currencies, trade, and debt are really just layers of a system built on confidence. When that confidence shifts, entire markets reprice overnight.

---

**The Core Concept (5-Minute Explainer):**

Forget GDP, inflation, and interest rates for a moment. Think of the global economy as a **giant trust network** — and money is just the token we agree has value because everyone else agrees it does.

Here's the first principle: **The world economy runs on layers of trust, and when trust breaks, everything re-prices.**

Concrete example: The US dollar isn't valuable because of gold backing — it's valuable because Saudi Arabia prices oil in dollars, Japan holds a trillion in Treasuries, and you can buy almost anything with it. That's a trust stack, not a "real" value. When Russia got cut off from SWIFT in 2022, it wasn't just sanctions — it was a crack in the trust layer. The system repriced: gold rallied, Bitcoin hit $70k, and the Fed had to swap lines with 16 central banks simultaneously.

Another: The Euro isn't a "real" currency — it's 20 countries agreeing to share monetary policy while running 20 different fiscal policies. That's like 20 roommates sharing one bank account but each spending differently. When Greece nearly blew up in 2015, it wasn't just a Greek crisis — it was a test of whether the trust layer held. The ECB's "whatever it took" moment wasn't economics — it was a trust patch.

The vivid mental model: **The global economy is a multi-layered trust stack:**
- Layer 1: Commodities (real stuff — oil, wheat, gold)
- Layer 2: Currencies (agreed value — dollar, euro, yuan)
- Layer 3: Debt (promised future value — bonds, credit)
- Layer 4: Derivatives (bets on bets — options, CDS)
- Layer 5: Digital tokens (new trust experiments — crypto, stablecoins)

Each layer is less "real" than the one below. When trust flows up the stack, everything inflates. When trust breaks, it cascades down — fast.

**The Counterintuitive Truth:**

What everyone gets wrong: **Debt isn't a bug — it's the feature.** Popular understanding says debt is bad, surpluses are good, and "living within your means" is virtuous. Reality? The entire global economy runs on debt. US national debt is $34 trillion. That's not a crisis — it's the *operating system*. Every dollar of debt is someone else's asset. When the US "defaults" on inflation, Japanese pensioners, Chinese exporters, and your 401k all feel it.

The gap between popular understanding and reality: People think central banks control interest rates. They don't — they *influence* them. The 10-year Treasury yield is set by thousands of traders betting on future growth, inflation, and trust. The Fed Funds Rate is just the overnight rate. When the Fed "prints money" (QE), it's not creating wealth — it's swapping one trust layer (bonds) for another (bank reserves). The magic trick: if everyone believes the new layer is as good as the old, nothing changes. If they don't, everything changes.
