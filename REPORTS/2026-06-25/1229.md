## 💡 Cross-Domain Insight of the Day

**The memory bottleneck is the throughline connecting AI, Apple, geopolitics, and space.**

Micron's revenue explosion is driven by AI's insatiable memory demand. Apple is raising prices because that same memory crunch hits consumer electronics. The Iran war disrupted Gulf trade routes that carry these components. And NASA finding water in space matters because the next frontier of computing — space-based data centers — will need memory too.

**The pattern:** We are in a *materials-constrained* technology cycle, not a capital-constrained one. The limiting factor isn't money (there's plenty) — it's physical inputs: memory chips, energy, water, rare earths. Every investment thesis should be stress-tested against this question: *does this depend on abundant cheap inputs that no longer exist?*

The winners of the next 5 years won't be the companies with the best AI models. They'll be the companies that secured the physical inputs — memory fabrication, energy generation, water rights, supply chain logistics. **Pick the bottleneck, not the breakthrough.**

Most people think the crash was about fraud—Do Kwon's Terra/LUNA, SBF's FTX, Celsius. Those were the headlines. But the *structure* that made the fraud inevitable was **composable leverage**: the ability to take an asset, borrow against it, use the loan as collateral for another loan, and repeat. In DeFi, this happened in seconds via smart contracts. On centralized exchanges like FTX, it happened through customer rehypothecation—lending out your deposits to fund risky bets.

Terra's "algorithmic stablecoin" was the detonator. It promised a $1 peg maintained not by reserves but by a mint/burn arbitrage with its sister token LUNA. When confidence cracked on May 7, 2022, the death spiral was mathematically unstoppable: LUNA minted into infinity to defend the peg, hyperinflating the token from $119 to $0.000001 in five days. $40 billion vanished. But the *blast radius*—Three Arrows Capital, Celsius, Voyager, BlockFi—came from the leverage woven through all of them. When one domino fell, they couldn't unwind positions because the *liquidity* didn't exist. Mark-to-market became mark-to-myth.

By November, FTX's collapse revealed the second principle: **opacity is leverage's oxygen**. SBF used ~$8 billion of customer funds for Alameda trading. No one could see the hole until withdrawals started. The lesson: in any system where you can't verify reserves, you are unsecured creditor, not customer.

**The Counterintuitive Truth:**
Everyone thinks "crypto is different because it's decentralized." The 2022 winter proved the opposite: **crypto's peak centralization risk exceeded traditional finance's**. When Lehman fell, collateral was messy but real—buildings, bonds, receivables. When 3AC fell, the "collateral" was tokens they'd minted themselves, or locked positions in illiquid DeFi pools. The decentralization was theater; the risk concentration was real. FTX was literally a single entity doing what banks do (fractional reserve) without any of the constraints (capital requirements, FDIC insurance, regulatory exams).
