## 📚 History Repeats: Lessons from Past Bubbles & Crashes  

### Why This Specific Aspect Matters  
The current AI wave is built on a web of APIs that let autonomous agents talk to each other. Understanding that pattern now tells investors where the next “fracture points” may appear.  

### The Core Concept (5‑Minute Explainer)  
A **bubble** forms when optimism about a new technology outpaces the underlying economic fundamentals, and participants rely on **implicit trust** that the connections holding the system together will stay intact. In the 1999 dot‑com boom, investors trusted that every internet company’s growth would be sustainable because the “network effect” seemed invincible—until the reality of profit‑less pipelines collapsed.  

The **core lesson** is that **interdependence without transparent, resilient governance creates systemic fragility**. When agents (or financial instruments) are linked by APIs, data feeds, or derivatives, a failure in one node can cascade instantly across the network. The antidote is **explicit standards, clear ownership, and auditability**—the same mechanisms that kept the 2008 banking system from total collapse after regulators forced banks to hold more capital and disclose exposures.  

### The Counterintuitive Truth  
Most people think a bubble bursts because “the hype dies down.” In reality, **the burst is triggered by the failure of the underlying connections**, not by a simple loss of sentiment. The 2000 tech bubble didn’t end because people stopped believing in the internet; it ended because the **API‑like relationships between over‑valued startups and their cash‑flow‑starved parents** collapsed, exposing the insolvency of many firms. The same dynamic is at play today: the AI boom will be tested not by a sudden drop in excitement but by **whether middleware and data‑layer governance can handle the load of billions of inter‑agent transactions**.
