## 📚 History Repeats: Lessons from Past Bubbles & Crashes  

**Why This Specific Aspect Matters:**  
Today’s AI‑driven rally feels familiar: euphoric headlines, soaring valuations, and a flood of capital chasing the next “platform.” Understanding the anatomy of past bubbles lets you spot when optimism is hardening into speculation—so you can protect gains, avoid buying at the peak, and position for the inevitable mean‑reversion.  

---

### The Core Concept (5‑Minute Explainer)  
A bubble isn’t just “prices too high.” It’s a self‑reinforcing loop of **credit expansion → asset‑price rise → collateral‑value rise → more credit**.  

1. **Seed:** A genuine innovation (railroads, internet, smartphones) creates real productivity gains.  
2. **Narrative Shift:** Storytellers (analysts, media, influencers) amplify the idea that “this time is different,” pushing expected returns far beyond fundamentals.  
3. **Leverage Feed‑back:** Banks and margin lenders accept the inflated assets as collateral, letting buyers borrow more to buy more—driving prices up further.  
4. **Euphoria & Herding:** Fear of missing out (FOMO) overrides valuation discipline; even skeptics buy to avoid looking foolish.  
5. **Trigger:** A shock—rate hike, fraud reveal, or a slowdown in earnings—makes marginal borrowers unable to service debt.  
6. **Crash:** Forced selling hits leveraged positions, collateral values fall, credit tightens, and the loop reverses violently.  

**Concrete example:** The 1990s dot‑com bubble. Real productivity from the web was ~1‑2% GDP boost, yet Nasdaq rose 400% (1995‑2000) on hopes of “eyeballs” monetization. Margin debt on NASDAQ stocks peaked at ~$150 bn; when earnings failed to justify valuations, margin calls forced a 78% collapse by 2002.  

---

### The Counterintuitive Truth  
**Popular belief:** “Bubbles are caused by irrational investors.”  
**Reality:** The irrationality is *systemic*—it’s the **credit‑collateral feedback** that makes rational actors behave irrationally. Even a sober CFO will lever up if the bank treats an overpriced asset as risk‑free collateral. The bubble is a *market‑institutional* phenomenon, not just a crowd‑psychology glitch.
