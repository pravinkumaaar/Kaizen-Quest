## 📚 Day 5: Pattern Recognition — How to Spot Bubbles Early

**Why This Specific Aspect Matters:**
We're living through a moment where AI capex is approaching dot-com levels, retail options activity is surging, and "this time it's different" is the consensus narrative. Spotting bubbles *before* they pop isn't about being bearish — it's about capital preservation and positioning. The skill separates those who build generational wealth from those who lose it.

---

**The Core Concept (5-Minute Explainer):**

A bubble isn't just "prices going up." It's a **specific psychological and structural pattern** that repeats with eerie consistency across centuries — from Dutch tulips (1637) to Beanie Babies (1999) to crypto (2021) to today's AI infrastructure spending.

Here's the anatomy, stripped to its skeleton:

**Phase 1 — Displacement:** Something genuinely new and valuable appears. Real innovation. Real opportunity. (Railway raising $100M for AI-native cloud infrastructure — legitimate.)

**Phase 2 — Euphoria:** Prices detach from fundamentals. Everyone's making money. Skeptics are mocked. Narrative shifts from "this is valuable" to "this is *the only thing* that matters. (Sound familiar? Every earnings call is now an "AI story.")

**Phase 3 — Warning Signs:** Insiders start selling. Leverage increases. Quality of new entrants deteriorates. (When your Uber driver gives you a stock tip — classic.)

**Phase 4 — Panic & Crash:** The music stops. Those who borrowed most suffer most. But here's the key — **the innovation survives.** The internet didn't die in 2001. The *companies* died. Amazon emerged stronger.

The pattern isn't about the technology — it's about **human behavior meeting asymmetric information.**

---

**The Counterintuitive Truth:**

Everyone thinks spotting bubbles means predicting *when* they'll burst. **Wrong.** The real skill is recognizing *which phase you're in* and adjusting position sizing accordingly.

Here's what most get wrong: **You don't need to know when the top is. You need to know when risk/reward shifts from asymmetric upside to asymmetric downside.**

In 1999, you didn't need to short Cisco at $80. You just needed to stop buying at 100x earnings with no path to profitability. The bubble was obvious in *retrospect* — but the signals were visible in real-time:

- **Valuation disconnect:** Price up 300%, earnings flat
- **Narrative dependency:** "New paradigm" = old words for "I can't justify this with math"
- **Universal participation:** When *everyone* is in, who's left to buy?
- **Insider behavior:** Executives selling while telling you to hold

Today? Watch for: AI companies with no revenue but massive capex, "AI-washing" in earnings calls, and retail FOMO into leveraged ETFs. The pattern isn't broken — it's *loading.*
